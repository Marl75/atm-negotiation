Airbus challenge #2 · adoption

Monday morning at DFW approach

What changes for the approach supervisor on the first shift with the tool, what it decides, what it never decides, and what has to be true before it leaves the hackathon.

A disrupted evening, before and after

Same Envoy 3439 from Mobile, 45 minutes late because its inbound aircraft is late. Today, the gate conflict is discovered when the aircraft is already on final.

TODAY
Phone tree Tower calls the airline ramp, ramp calls dispatch, dispatch calls the crew. Gate G10 turns out to be busy until 17:29 for an arrival at 16:55. The aircraft waits on the taxiway; the next leg leaves late.
T−110
The row arrives The tool reads the flight, its inbound aircraft and the limits of DFW (4 runways, 150 gates, 3 spare, 45-minute minimum turnaround).
TURN 1
Three agents, three domains ATC offers the slot, the pilot accepts, ground rejects: G10 is busy. The failed rule and its source are on screen.
TURN 2
Ground proposes G12 Decided 110 minutes ahead, well above the 20-minute gate-change notice. All three agents accept.
T−105
The supervisor decides One screen: plan, every check with its source, each agent's concerns. Approve or reject with a reason; the decision is written into the log.

The tool does

  • Collect the three domains' constraints on one disrupted flight, in at most three turns.
  • Check every proposal against fixed rules: wake separation, fuel reserve, crew duty, gates, curfews.
  • Turn airborne holding into a ground hold at the origin when that is better.
  • Say why when there is no agreement, and which rule blocks.
  • Escalate when no legal plan exists, instead of forcing one.

The tool never does

  • Apply a plan without the supervisor's approval.
  • Let a language model decide whether a plan is legal: the rules do.
  • Extend a crew's duty time or pick a reserve crew: that stays with the airline.
  • Replace the controller in tactical, final-approach decisions.
  • Use information that only exists after the flight.

Rollout

Weeks 1–4

Shadow mode. The tool runs on every disrupted arrival but shows nothing to operators. Compare its plans with what was actually decided.

Weeks 5–8

Advisory. The supervisor sees the plan and the checks, decides as today, and records agree / disagree with a reason.

Week 9 on

Approve-to-apply for gate changes and ground holds only. Crew and runway decisions stay manual until the crew and capacity feeds are live.
